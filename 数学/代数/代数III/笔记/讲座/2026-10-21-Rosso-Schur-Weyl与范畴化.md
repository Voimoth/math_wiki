# Marc Rosso：Interplays between symmetric groups and general linear groups

> 题目：*Interplays between symmetric groups and general linear groups: from classical to quantum, with a view towards categorification*
> 讲者：Marc Rosso（Université Paris Cité）
> 时间：2026-10-21（三）16:00–17:00，五教 5106

原稿把 $\mathfrak{S}_n$ 记作 $G_n$（$G_\infty=\bigcup_nG_n$）；下文按原句保留 $G_n$ 的写法。

## Symmetric group / general linear group

Symmetric group $G_n$ ; General linear group $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$, $k$ field
$$B=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}*&*\\0&*\end{pmatrix}\right\},\qquad U=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&*\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\right\}.$$
$G_n\subset\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ as permutation matrices.

For $k=\mathbb F_p$ — conséquences : $\exists p$-Sylow from any finite group $G\subset G_{|G|}$.

## Bruhat decomposition

$$\mathrm{GL}_n(k)=B\,G_n\,B .$$

Representations of groups : $\rho:G\to\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ morphisms.

Group algebra $k[G]=\mathcal F(G,k)$, convolution product. $\delta_g$ (delta function), $\delta_g\cdot\delta_{g'}=\delta_{gg'}$.

Bruhat : consider bi-invariant functions on $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ w.r.t. $B$ :
$$\mathcal H_n=\{f:\mathrm{GL}_n(k)\to k,\ f(bgb')=f(g),\ \forall b,b'\in B\}.$$
$\forall w\in\mathfrak{S}_n$, $BwB$, $T_w$ its characteristic function.

Result : For $k=\mathbb F_p$, $\mathcal H_n$ generated by $T_i:=T_{s_i}$, $s_i=(i\ i+1)$ ;
relations :
$$(\star)\qquad\begin{cases}T_i^2=(p-1)T_i+p,\\[2pt] T_iT_j=T_jT_i\quad(|i-j|\ge2),\\[2pt] T_iT_{i+1}T_i=T_{i+1}T_iT_{i+1}.\end{cases}$$

If "$p=1$" get permutation of $G_n$.

In general, for $q\in\mathbb C^*$ :
$$\mathcal H_n(q)=\{T_i,\ 1\le i\le n-1,\ \text{relations }(\star)\ \ p\leftarrow q\}.$$

## Classical : Schur-Weyl duality

Connection between reps of $G_n$ and $\mathrm{GL}_d(\mathbb C)$.

1) $G_n$ : irreducible reps are parametrized by partitions of $n$, $\lambda=(\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_n)$, $V_\lambda$.

2) $\mathrm{GL}_d(\mathbb C)$ : classification of f.d. reps, highest weight.
Consider restriction to diagonal matrices $\begin{pmatrix}t_1&&{ \\& } ;\ddots&\\&&t_n\end{pmatrix}$ which you try to simultaneously diagonalize.
For $\lambda=(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n)\in\mathbb Z^n$ :
$$\chi_\lambda:\begin{pmatrix}t_1&&{ \\& } ;\ddots&\\&&t_n\end{pmatrix}\longmapsto t_1^{\lambda_1}\cdots t_n^{\lambda_n}.$$
$V=\bigoplus V_\lambda$. $V$ is completed by the "highest" $\lambda$, $\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_n$.

The rep of $\mathrm{GL}_d(\mathbb C)$ with h.w. $\lambda$ is determined by "character" $g\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(\rho(g))$.
Restricted to diagonal matrices $\begin{pmatrix}t_1&&{ \\& } ;\ddots&\\&&t_d\end{pmatrix}$, it will be a polynomial in $t_1,\dots,t_d$, which is symmetric :
$$s_\lambda=\frac{\det\bigl(t_i^{\lambda_j+d-j}\bigr)}{\det\bigl(t_i^{d-j}\bigr)} .$$

**détails.**

Consider standard rep of $\mathrm{GL}_d(\mathbb C)$ in $\mathbb C^d=V$.
Take $V^{\otimes n}$ where $G$ acts $g(v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes v_n)=g(v_1)\otimes\cdots\otimes g(v_n)$.
Observe $G_n$ acts on $V^{\otimes n}$ by permuting the vectors :
$$w(v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes v_n)=v_{w(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{w(n)} .$$
$\forall g\in\mathrm{GL}_d$, $\forall w\in G_n$ : $g$ and $w$ commute.

Thm. (Schur-Weyl) They are exactly commutant of each other.
$$(\mathbb C^d)^{\otimes n}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\ \text{partitions}}V_\lambda\otimes W_\lambda,$$
$V_\lambda$ for $G_n$, $W_\lambda$ ind rep for $\mathrm{GL}_d$.

## Hopf algebra structure

A nice way to have tensor products of representations.

Let $(A,m)$ associative alg. $\Delta:A\to A\otimes A$ algebra morphism :
$$\begin{CD}
A @>{\Delta}>> A\otimes A\\
@V{\Delta}VV @VV{\Delta\otimes\mathrm{Id}}V\\
A\otimes A @>>{\mathrm{Id}\otimes\Delta}> A^{\otimes3}
\end{CD}$$
$\varepsilon:A\to k$ counit ("cogèbre").
$A=k[G]$, $\Delta(g)=g\otimes g$.

## Symmetric functions

Consider $k$ field or $\mathbb Z$. $x=(x_1,\dots,x_n,\dots)$.
Formal power series $f(x)=\sum_{\text{finite support}}c_\lambda x^\lambda$.

$R(x)=\{f,\ \text{degré borné}\}$ : if $\alpha_1+\cdots+\alpha_n>d$, $x^\alpha=0$.

from an algebra on which $G_\infty$ acts : $G_\infty=\bigcup_nG_n$, $G_1\subset G_2\subset\cdots\subset G_n$.

Then
$$\Lambda(x):=R(x)^{G_\infty}=\Bigl\{f=\sum c_\alpha x^\alpha \ \text{s.t.}\ c_\alpha=c_\beta\ \text{if}\ \beta=w(\alpha),\ w\in G_\infty\Bigr\}.$$

ex. $\lambda=(\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_n\ge0)$ :
$$m_\lambda=\sum_{\alpha\sim\lambda}x^\alpha\qquad\text{symmetric monomial},$$
$$m_{(2,1)}=x_1^2x_2+x_1x_2^2+x_1^2x_3+x_1x_3^2+\cdots$$

Prop. The $m_\lambda$'s form a basis of $\Lambda(x)$.

Other examples : $S_\lambda$ "Schur symmetric functions" : $\exists!\,s_\lambda\in\Lambda(x)$ s.t. when we put $x_{n+1}=x_{n+2}=\cdots=0$ one gets the Schur polynomial which gives character of $\mathrm{GL}_n$.
Prop : The $s_\lambda$'s are also a basis.

**Coproduct.**

Idea : $\mathbb N\times\mathbb N\hookrightarrow\mathbb N$, $\Delta:\Lambda(x)\to\Lambda(x)\otimes\Lambda(x)$.
Idea : $R(x)\otimes R(y)\hookrightarrow R(x,y)$ ; $\Lambda(x)\otimes\Lambda(y)=R(x,y)^{G_\infty\cdot G_\infty}$.
$f\in\Lambda(x)$ : choose a bijection $(x_1,x_2,\dots)\mapsto(x_1,x_2,\dots;y_1,y_2,\dots)$ ;
$$\Lambda(x)\longrightarrow\Lambda(x,y)\longrightarrow\Lambda(x)\otimes\Lambda(y).$$

$$\Delta m_{(2,1)}(x,y)=x_1^2x_2+x_1x_2^2+\cdots+x_1^2y_1+x_1y_1^2+x_1^2y_2+x_1y_2^2+\cdots+y_1^2y_2+y_1y_2^2+\cdots$$
$$=m_{(2,1)}(x)+m_{(2)}(x)m_{(1)}(y)+m_{(1)}(x)m_{(2)}(y)+m_{(2,1)}(y)$$
$$\Delta m_\lambda(x)=\sum_{\mu\cup\nu=\lambda}m_\mu\otimes m_\nu .$$

Thm. On the basis given by Schur fcts $s_\lambda$ :
$$s_\lambda s_\mu=\sum_\nu c_{\lambda\mu}^\nu s_\nu,\qquad \Delta s_\nu=\sum_{\lambda,\mu}c_{\lambda\mu}^\nu\,s_\lambda\otimes s_\mu$$
— some structure coeff for $\Delta$ and mult. ; $c_{\lambda\mu}^\nu\in\mathbb N$.

On $\Lambda(x)$ there is an inner product of $s_\lambda$ o.n.b. ; $m$ et $\Delta$ adjoints.

## A realization of $\Lambda(x)$

Consider the chain $G_1\subset G_2\subset\cdots\subset G_n$, $G_i\times G_j\hookrightarrow G_{i+j}$.
Call $R[G_n]=\mathbb Z[\text{Irr rep}]$ (representation ring).
2 operations : Restriction
$$R[G_{i+j}]\to R[G_i\times G_j]=R[G_i]\otimes R[G_j],$$
Induction
$$R[G_i]\otimes R[G_j]\to R[G_{i+j}].$$

$$A=\bigoplus_{j\ge1}R[G_j].$$
Induction provides product ; Restriction provides $\Delta:A\to A\otimes A$,
$$R[G_n]\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i+j=n}R[G_i]\otimes R[G_j].$$

Thm. There is a Hopf alg isomorphism
$$A\longrightarrow\Lambda(x)$$
$$\text{repn } G_n\longmapsto e_n=\sum_{i_1<\cdots<i_n}x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_n};\qquad \lambda\in\text{Part}_n:\ \text{rep } V_\lambda\ \text{of}\ G_n\longmapsto s_\lambda .$$

## Quantization

Come back to Hecke alg $\mathcal H_n(q)$.
It acts on each $(\mathbb C^d)^{\otimes n}$ as follows. $s_i=(i\ i+1)$, it acts only on positions $i,i+1$ :
$$v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes v_i\otimes v_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_n\longmapsto v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{i+1}\otimes v_i\otimes\cdots\otimes v_n .$$
$$T_i(e_k\otimes e_l)=\begin{cases}q\,e_k\otimes e_l,&k=l,\\ e_l\otimes e_k,&k>l,\\ e_l\otimes e_k+(q-q^{-1})\,e_k\otimes e_l,&k<l.\end{cases}$$
Introduce $(e_1,\dots,e_d)$ basis of $\mathbb C^d$.

There is a "deformation" or "quantization" of $\mathrm{GL}_d(\mathbb C)$ or rather of its enveloping algebra of $\mathfrak{gl}_d(\mathbb C)=M_d(\mathbb C)$, which has a Hopf alg structure, which acts in $\mathbb C^d$ as in classical situation, but due to non trivial $\Delta:V_q\to V_q\otimes V_q$ provides a Schur-Weyl duality with $\mathcal H_n(q)$.
